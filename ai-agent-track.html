About the track

Build trust into an emerging ecosystem.

AI agents increasingly operate through shared protocols, tool interfaces, identity systems, and open-source frameworks. This track invites practical contributions that make those shared foundations more secure, private, interoperable, accountable, and resilient.

Projects may study, test, extend, or build on open AI-agent standards and ecosystems, including NLIP and other open protocols. Contributions can address individual components or risks that emerge when independently developed agents, tools, services, and users interact.

Project directions

Focus areas

The examples below describe the track's scope. Participants may propose other hands-on projects that advance effective and trustworthy open-source AI agent ecosystems.

  • Secure, reliable interoperability among agents, tools, and services
  • Agent identity, authentication, authorization, and access control
  • Safe delegation, capability boundaries, and least-privilege design
  • Privacy, consent, provenance, and information-flow controls
  • Security of tool use, discovery, composition, and dependencies
  • Threat modeling, adversarial testing, fuzzing, and verification
  • Accountability, auditability, transparency, and governance
  • Reference implementations, benchmarks, datasets, and developer tools
